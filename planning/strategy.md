## 3. Go-to-market: the funnel

**Scan for fun. Stay to list. Pay to give.** Most people only ever use scan-to-gift, and every scan is free marketing. A steady share find gift lists, and a share of those become loyal concierge customers (D32, D33).

### The primary use case: buy me a drink (D32)
1. A college student wears a cool shirt (or other clothing) with a QR code.
2. Someone scans it and buys them a drink, **any drink**, in seconds and with no sign-up first.
3. It's anonymous through our platform's payment links. The sender can choose to be found with a profile or a selfie.
4. Both now have accounts. The wearer wants more gear, friends want shirts, and both are one tap from starting a gift list.

Party gear (sashes, tiaras) for bachelor and bachelorette parties is a second audience. Different versions (coffee, 21+ only) are planned later.

### Other use cases
| Use case | Product | Where it leads |
|---|---|---|
| Bride and groom party gear | Scan-to-gift | A wedding is the biggest gift list there is |
| Pay-it-forward plushie (our own collectible characters) or cookie, for college students and young moms | Scan-to-gift | Each link in the chain is a new paying user; new characters bring collectors back |
| QR greeting card that can't be stolen from the mail | Scan-to-gift | The sender is a giver, the natural concierge audience |
| The list keeper | Gift lists | Every guest who claims an item visits the platform |
| The forgetful giver | Gift Concierge | Uses the lists of the people they buy for; every gift shows the recipient the platform |
| The family on autopilot | Gift Concierge | Every occasion runs itself, and the picks get better each year |

### Handoffs between products
Each is a designed moment in the product, not left to chance.

| The moment | What we offer | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| You just bought a stranger a drink | "Start a list in one tap." | Gift lists |
| Someone bought you a drink | "Get your own shirt", "Make a list for your birthday" | More scans |
| You claimed an item on a friend's list | "Let the concierge remember their birthday." | Gift Concierge |
| A concierge gift arrived for you | "Make a list so next year's gift is perfect." | Gift lists |
| You're giving and the person has a list | The concierge suggests gifts from the list | Better ideas |
| Your list's gifts have arrived | A thank-you reminder, with a QR card option | Scan-to-gift |

### Growth loops
- **Wear it:** every shirt or sash in public is an ad, and every scan is a new account.
- **Pass it on:** every plushie or cookie paid forward and every card sent reaches a new person.
- **Share it:** every shared list brings its guests.
- **Give it:** every concierge gift introduces the recipient.

## 4. The final stage: the trust ladder (D33)

Each step earns more trust, so customers share more.

| Step | You share | You get |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scan-to-gift: a drink for a stranger | A payment | A fun moment |
| 2. Gift lists: a list of your own | What you'd like | Gifts you actually want, and friends' lists |
| 3. Gift Concierge: profiles of your people | Loved ones' dates, budgets, addresses, a few answers | Ideas that fit, on time, one-tap approval |
| 4. Gift Concierge: teach it their vibe | A guided tour of each person's social media, pointing out the posts that show what they're into | Gifting on autopilot, needing less input each year |

How the concierge's intelligence works (matches design §3.1):
- **It learns:** social posts the user shows it, lists, notes and answers become facts about the person, each with its source, all editable.
- **It thinks:** fresh ideas each occasion from everything it knows plus gift history, only things it can buy and deliver on time.
- **It gets better:** approvals, rejection reasons and reactions teach it. A loved one's own gift list feeds it directly once lists connect.

## 5. Launch sequence

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **0. Friends and family (now)** | Gift lists soft launch; Gift Concierge MVP for the owner, Jim and 1–2 friends; pick one platform name |
| **1. Seed a campus** | Free buy-me-a-drink shirts to friends' and family's college networks on one campus. Learn whether people scan, pay and come back. Bridge every scanner to a list. |
| **2. Events and seasons** | Bachelor and bachelorette gear for wedding season, with wedding lists. QR cards for Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, graduations and Christmas. Plushie chains with seasonal characters. Cookie promotions with local bakeries. |
| **3. Convert and charge** | Concierge public launch with premium automation. Offer it to list guests and frequent givers first. Help loyal customers build profiles, including the social media tour, then automate their recurring gifts. Per-gift fees. |

## 6. Money model (direction, not decided) (D34)
- **Free:** managing and sharing gift lists.
- **Premium:** automated giving and receiving (the concierge).
- **Per gift:** a fee on scan-to-gift and custom gifts. Zinc's $1 per order can be passed to the buyer.

Billing stays out of MVP scope.

## 7. What we measure
One number per handoff: scans per item; scans that become a paid gift; scanners who create an account; accounts that start a list; lists shared and guests who join; list users who try the concierge; concierge users who pay; loved ones' profiles filled in per customer; recurring gifts that run with one-tap approval or less. Targets are set after Phase 1.

## 8. Assumptions to test
- Strangers will scan and pay, and it feels fun, not creepy.
- Paying for a drink takes seconds with no sign-up first.
- A payments provider can handle payouts to wearers and the payment rules **[Verify]**.
- People who scan for fun will care about gift lists.
- Loyal customers will fill in loved ones' profiles and walk the concierge through their social media.
- A plushie can be cheap enough to send and still feel collectible, with our own designs.
- One brand ties it together, so people recognize all three products as one place.
